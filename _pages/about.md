## Project overview

Cities are focal points of human and infrastructure vulnerability to climate hazards, and are central to climate mitigation and adaptation. Yet global Earth system models still represent urban areas in limited ways, making it harder to project local urban climates and assess adaptation options.

The Community Earth System Model (CESM) currently represents urban areas using three built types—tall building district, high density and medium density—based on 2004 population data. These broad categories do not capture the diversity of urban form and surface properties. This project will bring the **Local Climate Zones (LCZs)** classification, widely used in observational urban climate studies, into global Earth system modelling.

The team will develop global urban surface data for ten LCZ built types, implement an LCZ scheme in CESM and evaluate urban climate projections over the next 50 years. The work will also assess adaptation options such as white roofs. Methods, models, data and research outputs are intended to be freely accessible to model developers, urban planners, policymakers and other communities.

### Work packages

1. **Global urban surface data:** develop and integrate LCZ-based global urban surface parameters into the Community Land Model – Urban (CLMU), the urban component of CESM.
2. **Model development and evaluation:** implement the LCZ scheme in CESM and validate simulations across different configurations.
3. **Climate adaptation:** model urban adaptation, with a focus on changes to urban surface albedo, such as white roofs.

The project aims to improve understanding of urban heat islands and climate extremes, provide data that can support other community models, and help inform resilient and equitable adaptation strategies.
